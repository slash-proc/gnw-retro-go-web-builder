# Library: how it is browsed

Three of the ten boards. These vary one thing: **how much of the screen the cover art gets, and
what the primary surface for picking a game is.** Generated by `build-browsing.py`.

Everything else is held still on purpose. Same chrome, same console chips, the same twelve games
lifted row for row from the approved `mockups/Roms.dc.html`, the same three option sections, the
same dock. A set that varies two things at once cannot be compared.

**The console filter stays a horizontal chip strip.** `overview-v2/README-library.md` argues it
into a rail and that argument may well win, but it costs 244px of width and these three boards are
about width. Holding it still keeps the comparison clean. The rail question belongs to a different
board.

**Four of the twelve fixtures have no cover art** (`Ball`, `Back to the Future`, `Blow'em Out`,
`Alfonzo's Arctic Adventure`). Every board draws them as they actually are. A Game and Watch and
homebrew library is mostly titles with no art, and a cover-led arrangement has to be judged on
those, not on the ones that happen to have a box scan.

---

## CarouselFirst

The coverflow is the page. Full width at the top, the selected cover large and centred, its
neighbours smaller and dimmed. Underneath, the selected title's three option sections sit side by
side at full width, and the games list is a 340px column on the right.

**For:** answering "what if the nicest thing on the screen got the most room". At 168x210 a cover
is actually recognisable, which is the whole argument for having one. All three option sections
fit on one row with no drawer, no tabs and no mode, so cheats are exactly as visible as cover art.

**Trades away:** the list. It is 340px wide and shows eight of 137 rows. Selecting fifty games for
an install means scrolling a narrow column while the largest thing on screen is art for the one
game you already picked. The Library is a bulk selection tool first and a browser second, and this
board inverts that.

**At risk from the checklist:** size per row survives but only just, in a cramped column. The BIOS
rows have nowhere sensible to go, and unknown-homebrew rows with their `Remove` would make the
narrow column worse.

## CoverGrid

No list and carousel split. Every game is a tile carrying its own checkbox and its own state pill.
The chips filter the grid; the selected tile drives a 360px options column on the right.

**For:** removing a real duplication rather than tidying it. Today the same game is drawn twice,
once as a row and once as a cover, and `getActionState` is rendered in both places. One surface
means one place to look and one place to click. It also scales without a redesign: at 150px the
grid wraps five across in the 972px column, so 137 games is 28 rows of scrolling.

**Trades away:** size and filename disappear from the browse surface. They exist only in the
options column, for the selected game, so "which of these is the 2MB one" becomes a per-game
question. On a medium with a hard budget that is a genuine loss, and the dock's projected total is
carrying more weight here than on any other board.

**At risk from the checklist:** the size column, the filename subline, BIOS rows, and unknown
homebrew. A tile is a poor row.

## DetailView

A mode. Picking a game opens it: cover at 240x300, metadata beside it, the three option sections as
full-width cards below, and the carousel kept as a strip along the bottom so the next game is one
click away.

**For:** room, and specifically room to grow. Additional options is the part the owner expects to
extend, and this is the only board in my three where a fourth and fifth section cost nothing. They
are two more cards down the page. Nothing is squeezed, nothing is behind a tab, and the carousel
finally has a job beyond being pleasant: it is how you move between games without leaving the mode.

**Trades away:** the selection context. The bulk work happens in the list view, and while you are
in here you cannot see what else is selected. The dock's running total is your only thread back to
the install you are assembling.

**At risk from the checklist:** nothing is lost, but everything list-shaped moves to the view you
came from, so this board only makes sense paired with one of the others rather than replacing it.

---

## Which one I would not ship

**CoverGrid.** It is the most attractive of the three and the least honest.

The grid only works if the tiles carry meaning, and a third of this library has no cover art. Five
dashed placeholders per row is not a browse surface, it is a list with the text removed. Worse, the
two facts a Library exists to weigh, size and total, are the two the grid drops: you are picking
what to write to a device with a hard byte budget, and the per-game number is gone from the surface
where you do the picking.

It is worth keeping on the canvas as the argument against itself. If cover art coverage ever gets
close to complete it becomes the obvious answer, and that is worth knowing.

If I had to pick one to build: **DetailView, paired with the existing list**, because it is the only
arrangement where the thing the owner says will grow can actually grow.

## Not verified

Nothing here has been seen rendered. There is no headless browser in this container and I did not
install one. What I did check mechanically: every board parses with no mis-nested or unclosed tags,
no em-dash or interpunct, no banned vocabulary, and the layout arithmetic of every fixed-width
column, carousel band and tile grid against the 1360px content width. That arithmetic caught one
real error: the grid wraps five across, not the six the note originally claimed.

Type sizes, vertical rhythm and whether the carousel band looks right at 210px are unseen.
